The Problem I Saw

The world grades you on paperwork.

Most companies are started to chase a market. This house was started to close a gap.

I'm first generation of everything — Orlando roots, Maryland finish. My diploma didn't come with a ceremony: I finished high school in Maryland by GED, 2008. No cap. No gown. No apologies. What it did come with was a view most people never get — I saw, up close, exactly how the world treats people whose credentials don't sparkle.

And here is what I saw and could not unsee: technology — the one industry that promised to be a meritocracy — quietly became the fastest gate in town. Built by the credentialed. Priced for the privileged. Explained to everybody else in a language designed not to be understood. People with real vision were left choosing between tools that insulted them and consultants who talked past them.

So I decided the gap was the opportunity. It was never really a tech gap. It was a trust gap — and trust is never built with resumes. It's built with receipts.
